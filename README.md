## Current canonical story

TurtleBlock AI is a public educational research project exploring how people and computational agents can **build, inhabit, question, compare, and revise ideas together** without collapsing human purpose into machine interpretation.

The project now includes:

- **WorldSpec** — inspectable, revisable representation between human meaning and computational construction;
- **Turtle Charter** — behavioral and agency boundaries for Turtle;
- **Turtle Terraria** — the expanded research environment that grew out of Turtle Lab;
- **Human + Turtle Terrarium** — human-driven inquiry and construction;
- **Recursive Turtle Terrarium** — explicitly synthetic self-play with no autonomous production authority;
- **Human Tamagotchi Terrarium + TurtleAsk** — a research condition in which Turtle may eventually seek genuine human perturbation through a separate consent/attention gate;
- **Selective Attention** — noticing is not permission to interrupt;
- **Plural Foregrounds** — shared worlds do not require machine-manufactured consensus;
- **Human Perturbation Translation** — human input must survive machine interpretation with provenance intact;
- **Branch Experience Comparison** — alternate builds can be compared without automatically selecting a winner;
- **Cross-Branch Perturbation** — one branch can lend another a question without merging its history;
- **Persistent Perturbation** — long-running machine initiative must remain learner-contestable and revocable;
- **Sanders Research Ontology** — immutable dissertation source layer plus later provenance-aware research concepts and operational mappings;
- **Research Library** — a public D1-backed catalog preserving photographed physical sources, editions, special notes, artifact provenance, and research connections;
- **Build Log + Next Edge** — completed work separated from the living future-facing research question.

The package remains deliberately conservative at **v0.1.0** until implemented and tested capability earns a larger version claim.

## Language rules that matter

### Turtle Lab → Turtle Terraria

The canonical transition is:

```text
Turtle Lab
   ↓ expanded into
Turtle Terraria
```

Do not describe this as “Terraria became Terraria.”

### Human and machine traces remain distinct

```text
human intention
  ≠ Turtle interpretation
  ≠ synthetic self-play
  ≠ WorldSpec state
  ≠ world observation
  ≠ scholarly source
  ≠ later ontology mapping
```

They may be related. They should not be silently collapsed.

### Build Log and Next Edge are different things

- **Build Log** = completed, committed work.
- **Wait a minute…** = an evidence-backed connection discovered around completed work.
- **Next Edge** = one open question beyond the latest completed work.
- **Possible Possibles** = alternate futures worth preserving.
- **X-factor** = explicit room for the not-yet-classifiable.

Current traditional X-factor language:

> **whoooo knooooowwwssssssssssss**

## Visual and editorial language

The public face should feel like the project:

- blocks, loops, arrows, worlds, terraria, branches, traces, questions;
- playful without becoming unserious;
- research-grounded without turning every doorway into a bibliography;
- provisional things may look provisional;
- failures and reversals remain visible when they teach something;
- “I don’t know yet” is acceptable interface language;
- learner agency should be visible in both prose and architecture;
- no claim of machine consciousness is required for interesting machine behavior;
- if documentation begins to sound like generic enterprise AI middleware, release a turtle into it.

## House rule

When the public profile, platform README, live site, research architecture, and repository state diverge, treat the divergence as a defect.

But do not solve that by making every surface identical.

Each surface should preserve the same **truth** while speaking for its own room.

> **DO NOT POLISH AWAY THE INTERESTING PARTS.**
